# Research report

**Task:** Evaluate whether multi-agent AI systems are worth using compared to a single LLM

## Summary  
Multi-agent AI systems provide distinct advantages in coordination, fault isolation, and scalability, but they also incur higher latency, operational costs, and complexity compared with single-large-language-model (LLM) pipelines. Benchmarks such as MultiAgentBench show that multi-agent setups can achieve higher coordination quality, yet controlled studies reveal that single-agent systems deliver better information-efficiency and lower computational overhead when token budgets are matched. For well-defined, sequential tasks, a single LLM remains simpler to govern and cost-effective, whereas multi-agent architectures become preferable when tasks grow in cross-functional complexity or when new domains must be added incrementally.

## Findings  

### Benchmarking and Performance  
MultiAgentBench evaluates multi-agent LLM systems across six interactive scenarios, reporting success-rate, F1-score, Pass@k, and a "progress rate" metric that captures both accuracy and coordination quality, whereas single-agent benchmarks typically report only accuracy-oriented scores such as Pass@k [1]. In a controlled study, single-agent systems (SAS) consistently achieved higher information-efficiency—better accuracy per token—than multi-agent architectures (MAS) when the total "thinking token" budget was matched [2, 3]. A simulated Mars-rover decision-support benchmark demonstrated that a single-agent LLM architecture achieved lower computational overhead and faster inference than a multi-agent orchestration pipeline that required sequential coordination steps among agents [4].

### Fault Isolation and Graceful Degradation  
Multi-agent setups can isolate failures: if one specialized agent crashes, the overall workflow can continue, allowing graceful degradation, whereas a single-agent system has a single decision engine whose failure can halt the entire process [5]. This isolation capability makes multi-agent systems more resilient in environments where continuous operation is critical.

### Expansion and Cost Efficiency  
Introducing specialist agents for new domains lets teams expand capabilities without retraining a monolithic model, making growth more manageable and often more cost-efficient over time [5]. Multi-agent architectures enable parallel execution, supporting highly scalable expansion as tasks or data are added [6]. However, multi-agent deployments multiply LLM calls, leading to higher latency and operational expense, and the coordination layer must be monitored, adding complexity [7]. Single-agent pipelines keep costs close to the price of a single model call because they avoid the coordination layer and multiple LLM invocations required by multi-agent designs [7].

### Governance and Manageability  
A single agent is simpler to govern and cost-effective for well-defined, sequential tasks, but becomes harder to manage as cross-functional complexity rises [5]. Multi-agent systems distribute responsibilities, which can simplify updates to individual components but introduce a coordination layer that must be monitored [7]. Thus, governance trade-offs favor single-agent solutions for straightforward workflows and multi-agent solutions for complex, evolving domains.
