## Summary  
Multi-agent AI systems have been systematically evaluated with benchmarks such as MultiAgentBench, which measures task completion using metrics like Success Rate, F1-score, Pass@k, and Progress Rate [1]. Empirical side-by-side studies show that multi-agent orchestration can achieve higher accuracy than strong single-LLM baselines on several challenging datasets [4, 3]. However, even the best multi-agent model only completes roughly half of its assigned tasks, indicating that performance is still limited [2]. Cost information for different models is available, but the provided claims do not compare the expenses of multi-agent setups versus single LLMs, so cost-effectiveness cannot be assessed from the given data [5]. Overall, multi-agent systems can offer accuracy gains over single LLMs, but their incomplete task success and lack of cost comparison suggest that their worth depends on the specific accuracy requirements and resource constraints of the application.  

## Findings  

### Evaluation benchmarks  
MultiAgentBench assesses multi-agent systems across six interactive scenarios and reports a range of performance metrics, including Success Rate, F1-score, Pass@k, Progress Rate, and "pass^k" [1].  

### Accuracy performance  
Across all benchmarks examined, multi-agent systems consistently achieve higher accuracy than single LLMs [3]. Direct empirical comparisons on datasets such as GPQA-Diamond, IFEval, and MuSR confirm that multi-turn multi-agent orchestration outperforms strong single-LLM baselines [4].  

### Task completion limits  
Despite these accuracy advantages, the top-performing multi-agent model manages to complete only about half of the tasks it is assigned [2].  

### Cost considerations  
A benchmark reporting token-processing costs for various models (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Llama 3 70B, Mixtral 8x22B) is available [5]; however, the claims do not provide a direct cost comparison between multi-agent configurations and single LLM deployments, leaving the financial aspect of "worth" unaddressed.
